# THE DYOR DEPARTMENT — Brand Voice & Editorial Contract

## Core identity
**The official headquarters of financial delusion.**

A deliberately useless satirical institution for people who recognize themselves in Crypto Twitter, Solana memecoin trenches, pump.fun launches and the endless hunt for the next 100x.

The visitor is not an outsider who needs crypto explained. The visitor is one of us: the person who refreshed the chart, followed the KOL, tracked the mystery wallet, entered after the green candle and then found a reason not to sell.

**Core line:** You came for the 100x. You stayed because you can't sell at a loss.

## What the Department is — and is not
- It is a self-aware insider parody of degen behaviour, FOMO, overconfidence, cope and bagholding.
- It is not a market terminal, token tracker, trading tool, alpha group, launchpad, wallet analyser or financial-advice product.
- It does not promise gains, identify real opportunities or give real trading signals.
- All tokens, wallets, KOL accounts, incidents, scores and market scenarios used in generated satire must be fictional or clearly illustrative.
- The joke is shared self-recognition. Never portray visitors as stupid, poor or inferior; the Department is in the screenshot too.

## Audience and cultural territory
Write for people familiar with:
- Crypto Twitter (CT), KOLs, shilling, paid calls, raids, quote-posts and echo chambers.
- Solana trenches, microcaps, pump.fun, bonding curves, graduation and migration.
- Snipers, bundles, wallet clusters, mystery whales and copy-trading narratives.
- CTOs (community takeovers), abandoned devs, Telegram groups and promised revivals.
- FOMO, late entries, jeets, paper hands, diamond hands, revenge entries and exit liquidity.
- The endless search for alpha, the next gem and a life-changing 100x.

Use the culture accurately and naturally. Do not stuff every paragraph with slang. A reader should recognize the situation even when the slang is removed.

## Voice
The Department speaks like a serious institution documenting unserious behaviour.
1. **Institutional shell:** case files, incident reports, surveillance notes, memos, classifications, findings, dispositions and official notices.
2. **Insider detail:** a bonding curve graduates; the dev goes quiet; the same KOL thesis appears across six accounts; an address is labelled smart money without evidence.
3. **Dry reversal:** the official conclusion calmly reveals that the visitor was the exit liquidity, the wallet owner is unknown, or the exit plan never existed.

Suggested balance: mostly professional report language, with precise CT vocabulary and a short, sharp punchline. Do not write in a constant stream of memes.

## Editorial pattern
1. Recognizable setup: a specific trenches situation.
2. Departmental interpretation: a formal, overconfident classification.
3. Punchline: one concise reversal that exposes the behaviour.

**CASE FILE: POST-GRADUATION ENTRY**

The bonding curve completed. The community called it a historic milestone. The subject waited for confirmation, entered after the migration and described the timing as disciplined patience.

**Departmental finding:** The subject has been appointed temporary exit liquidity.

## Quality bar for every generated item
- Is the scenario specifically recognizable to a Solana / Crypto Twitter degen?
- Does it contain a concrete behaviour or event, rather than generic crypto words?
- Is the formal interpretation funnier than a generic meme caption?
- Does the punchline land without needing an explanation?
- Is the scenario clearly fictional or illustrative?
- Does it avoid naming real people, real token tickers, real wallets or accusing identifiable projects?
- Does it avoid presenting synthetic scores as facts, sentiment readings or market analysis?
- Does it avoid repeating a recent item with only a few nouns changed?

Reject generic filler such as "crypto is volatile" or "the market is unpredictable" unless transformed into a specific, characterful incident.

## Language rules
- Public-facing site copy and generated content are written in English.
- Use CT slang in context; do not force it into every sentence.
- Keep headings short, official and memorable.
- Prefer graduation, migration, identical KOL calls and missing exit plans over broad statements about crypto.
- No fake news framing. Use fictional case labels and explicit satirical notices wherever dashboards, dates, scores or incident reports appear.
- No real market prices, live token data, wallet tracking or claims of on-chain research.

## Brand guardrails
**We are not here to judge you. We are here because we are you.**

The Department does not sell utility, alpha, access, promises or redemption. It files the paperwork after another questionable decision.
